# ATLAS — Briefing Document

**Purpose of this document:** This is a complete, plain-language brief about ATLAS. Hand it to ChatGPT (or any writer) so they can create accurate content — FAQs, video scripts, launch posts, captions — without guessing or inventing anything. Everything a writer needs to understand ATLAS is here. If a fact is not in this document, do not make it up.

---

## 1. What ATLAS is (in one line)

**ATLAS is a friendly on-the-floor helper that instantly finds the right Customer Experience guidance from Alshaya's own training library — in English or Arabic.**

Longer version: ATLAS is a simple search assistant that lives inside the **CX Hub**. A store or restaurant team member types a question in everyday words ("a customer is angry, what do I do?") and ATLAS instantly returns the most relevant answer, pulled straight from Alshaya's own Customer Experience training modules. It works on a phone, in two languages, and it always shows where the answer came from.

Think of ATLAS as a **knowledgeable colleague who has read every CX module and can point you to the right guidance in seconds** — not a robot that makes things up.

---

## 2. Why ATLAS exists (the problem it solves)

- Frontline teams have questions in the moment — on the shop floor, at the till, at the coffee bar — but there aren't always enough managers or area managers available to answer them.
- The CX training content already exists, but it's spread across many modules, decks and videos. Finding the exact answer quickly is hard.
- ATLAS closes that gap: it turns all of that training into something a team member can **search in seconds**, so good guidance is always within reach, even when no one is free to ask.

**One-line version:** ATLAS gives every team member instant access to the CX training answers they need, without waiting to find a manager.

---

## 3. What ATLAS is NOT (very important — this shapes the honest tone)

This section matters most. ATLAS should never be oversold as something it isn't.

- **ATLAS is NOT an AI chatbot like ChatGPT.** It does not "think," generate opinions, or invent answers. It does not write new content. It finds and shows the best match from a fixed, approved library.
- **ATLAS is NOT connected to the internet.** It cannot browse, search the web, or pull in outside information.
- **ATLAS does NOT know anything beyond the CX training library.** Ask it about the weather, football, or anything off-topic and it simply won't have an answer — and it will say so.
- **ATLAS does NOT collect data, track staff, or store conversations.** There are no accounts, no logins to use it, no monitoring, and no reporting on who asked what.
- **ATLAS is NOT a performance or monitoring tool.** It doesn't score anyone or report to management.
- **ATLAS does NOT replace managers, trainers, or the training itself.** It's a quick-reference helper that sits alongside them.
- **ATLAS canNOT see customer data, orders, POS, stock, or any live systems.** It only knows the training content.
- **ATLAS is NOT an app you download.** It's a simple web link that opens in a browser.

**Golden rule for all content:** never call ATLAS "AI" or imply it thinks. The honest, confident framing is: *"It's not AI making things up — it's your own training, made instantly searchable."*

## 4. How ATLAS works (simple explanation)

1. The team member opens ATLAS (a web link) and picks their division.
2. They type a question in normal, everyday language — English or Arabic.
3. ATLAS matches the question against the training library and returns the **single best answer** as a short, plain-language card.
4. Each answer shows:
   - a clear title and a short, practical answer,
   - the **source** it came from (e.g. "Source: CX Hub - Art of Selling - Conflict Resolution"),
   - a **Watch video** button when a related training video exists,
   - an **Open full module** button when there's a full module to read,
   - a couple of **related topics** to explore next.
5. If ATLAS can't find a good match, it honestly says so and suggests checking with a line manager or area manager.

**Key point for writers:** ATLAS understands loose wording. Someone can type "angry customer," "customer shouting at me," or "زبون غاضب" and still land on the right answer. It also handles both English and Arabic, including older wording habits.

## 5. The content model — our own library, our own content, nothing from outside

This is a core selling point and must be stated clearly and proudly:

- **Every single answer comes from Alshaya's own Customer Experience training modules.** Nothing is pulled from the internet. Nothing is invented by a machine.
- The content was written and curated by the **Customer Experience team** — the same team behind the CX Hub.
- Because the content is fixed and approved, answers are **consistent, accurate, and on-brand** every time. Two team members asking the same question get the same trusted answer.
- Every answer is **traceable** back to its source module, shown right under the answer.
- The library can grow over time — the CX team simply adds more content. The tool itself never changes.

**One-line version:** *Our content, our modules, our voice — nothing from outside, nothing made up.*
